## How to read a listing

Every tool lists the same fields:

- **Access label:** free tier, free, open source, or self-hosted. The label describes the free path, not quality.
- **What is free:** the useful part of the no-cost option, not just a sign-up trial.
- **Sign-up notes:** the account, app install, or verification step, plus whether a card is expected for the free path.
- **Limits:** credits, caps, watermarks, queues, account requirements, or paid gates.
- **Best for:** one honest job the tool does well.
- **Official link:** the provider's own site. No affiliate redirects.
- **Tags:** quick cues for search and scanning.
- **Last verified:** the date this entry was reviewed.

A free tool can still be a bad fit. It can change tomorrow, train on your input, restrict commercial output, or require hardware you do not have. Use the safety guide before you spend time or share data.

## A five-minute start

1. Pick **one job** you actually need to finish: "summarise a public report," "clean up a chapter," "make a square post for my business."
2. Pick **one tool** from the matching category. Use the "start here" pick if you are unsure.
3. Read its **sign-up notes** and **limits** before creating an account.
4. Try it with **invented or public material**.
5. Stop if it does not save time or improve the result. A smaller toolbox is easier to use.

For a gentler week-by-week route, use [Start here](start-here.md). If decision fatigue is the real obstacle, that page has a two-tool path built for low-energy days.

## The short safety version

- Never put patient information, client records, student records, passwords, API keys, identity documents, financial data, or unpublished manuscripts into a free account without permission and a genuine data agreement.
- Check the pricing page, the renewal screen, and who owns the output.
- Free access and commercial rights are different things.
- Health tools in this guide are for education only. Do not use them to diagnose, treat, triage, calculate doses, or make care decisions.
- Keep a backup method for accessibility and other essential tasks.
- Do not treat a confident AI answer as evidence. Open the source.

Read the full [free-tier safety checklist](free-tier-safety.md).
