# Free-tier safety checklist

Free AI tools can be genuinely useful. They can also be a masterclass in hidden costs: your data, your time, your publishing rights, your inbox, and eventually a subscription you forgot to cancel.

This checklist is the page to read **before** you create an account. It is written for a normal person with limited time, not for a procurement department.

> This is general educational information, not legal, financial, medical, or security advice. When the stakes are high, use the provider's current terms and a qualified professional.

## 1. Understand which kind of "free" you are getting

| Kind of free | What it usually means | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Permanent free tier** | A limited version that is meant to exist indefinitely | Current limits, model access, and whether the free tier is being downgraded |
| **Free account / free core** | Core features cost nothing; upgrades are optional | Which features are gated, output rights, watermarks, storage caps |
| **Time-limited trial** | Full or enhanced access for a set period | End date, card requirement, automatic renewal, cancellation method |
| **Credit-based free plan** | A monthly or one-time allowance of credits | Credit cost per action, rollover rules, what happens when credits run out |
| **Open-source software** | The code is available, usually at no licence cost | Licence, hardware, setup time, model downloads, maintenance |
| **Self-hosted** | You run the service yourself | Server costs, security, backups, exposure to the internet, updates |
| **"Free" in exchange for data** | You pay with content, usage data, or public posting | Training use, human review, retention, account deletion, opt-out |
| **Free for a group** | Free only for students, teachers, nonprofits, or a region | Verification, renewal, what happens when your status changes |

"Free" is not a price. It is a set of conditions. Find the conditions before you invest time.

## 2. The ten-minute pre-sign-up check

1. **Open the official pricing page.** Not a review site, not a social post, not a coupon page. Find the free plan and read the limits.
2. **Look for a card field.** If a card is required, treat the offer as a trial. Write down the end date and the cancellation steps before you start.
3. **Read the renewal screen.** Look for automatic renewal, annual billing, seat minimums, and taxes. Take a screenshot of the terms you agreed to.
4. **Check what happens at the limit.** Does the tool stop, degrade, watermark, delete access, or automatically charge an overage?
5. **Check output rights.** Can you use the result commercially? Do you need to credit the provider? Are generated images public by default?
6. **Check input and training use.** Will your prompts, uploads, or voice recordings be used to improve the service? Can you opt out? Does the free tier reserve broader rights than the paid tier?
7. **Set up account security.** Use a unique password, a password manager, and multi-factor authentication. Do not sign in with a shared password or an email account you are about to abandon.
8. **Find the export and delete controls.** If you cannot export your work, do not make the tool the only copy. If you cannot delete your data, assume it may persist.
9. **Test with invented material.** Use a fake chapter, a public document, or a harmless image before you upload something private.
10. **Decide how you will leave.** Note the cancellation path and any deadlines now, while you are thinking clearly. Calendar reminders beat heroic memory.

## 3. Privacy: what never goes into a free account

Unless you have clear permission, an appropriate data agreement, and a provider whose terms match the sensitivity of the material, do not upload:

- patient information or protected health information (PHI);
- client records, contracts, or confidential business data;
- student records, grades, private messages, or images of minors;
- passwords, passkeys, recovery codes, API keys, or access tokens;
- Social Security numbers, tax records, bank details, or payment card numbers;
- identity documents such as passports, licences, or medical records;
- biometric data, including faces and voices, without informed consent;
- unpublished manuscripts, patent material, or creative work you cannot afford to leak;
- anyone else's private information, even in a screenshot or background image.

Free accounts often have broad service rights, limited retention controls, and human review for quality or safety. A "private" chat may mean "not public," not "confidential" in the legal or clinical sense. Read the privacy policy and your workplace's rules.

### De-identification is harder than it looks

Removing a name is not enough. Dates, rare diagnoses, small towns, job titles, room numbers, and distinctive phrases can identify a person. Pixelated faces can sometimes be reconstructed. Audio contains voices, background conversations, and room echoes. If you would not put the file on a public noticeboard, do not assume a free AI tool is an appropriate home for it.

### Children and students

Extra care applies. Do not upload student work, behaviour notes, or images without the school's approval and the family's consent where required. Use district-approved tools for anything that is part of a student's record. A free classroom tool is not automatically compliant with your school's privacy obligations.

### Meetings, voices, and faces

Record only with permission, and know your local rules. "Everyone is okay with it" is not the same as informed consent. For a voice clone, face swap, or avatar, get explicit, documented permission from the person and anyone who owns the rights to the material. Never create sexual, misleading, or harassing synthetic media of a real person.

## 4. Account and payment safety

- **Use a password manager.** A unique password for every AI service matters more than a clever password you reuse.
- **Turn on multi-factor authentication** if the provider offers it. Prefer an app or hardware key over SMS when you can.
- **Check the URL.** Official domains matter. Lookalike sites buy ads and mimic login pages. Type the address or use a saved bookmark instead of trusting the first search result.
- **Do not share a login.** Seat limits and account-sharing rules are common. Shared accounts also blur who did what.
- **Beware social-login sprawl.** Signing in with Google or Apple is convenient but can grant access to profile data. Review permissions rather than clicking through.
- **Keep API keys out of everything.** An API key is a password for a paid service. Do not paste one into a chat, screenshot it, commit it to GitHub, or share it in a prompt. If you are experimenting with a developer tool, use environment variables and a key with spending limits.
- **Set billing alerts.** If you attach a card to a paid plan, set a low alert and know how to cancel. Free trials can convert silently.
- **Log out on shared devices.** Download any exports you need before you go.

## 5. Money traps and dark patterns

Watch for these phrases and patterns:

- **"Free forever"** with no limits listed. Find the actual caps.
- **"No credit card required"** followed by a card prompt at checkout. The first screen is marketing; the checkout is the deal.
- **"Unlimited"** subject to "fair use" or queue restrictions. Find the fair-use definition if your work depends on it.
- **Annual discount on a trial.** You may be agreeing to a year of billing.
- **Cancellation by phone or chat only.** That is a friction tactic. Confirm the process before signing up.
- **Credits that expire.** Check whether unused credits roll over.
- **Free plan removal after investment.** Keep your source files and a plan for exporting work if the plan changes.
- **Add-ons, seat upgrades, and "premium" models.** The free model may not be the one in the demo.

A useful rule: if you cannot explain how the company makes money, be extra careful about what you give it. Free tools are often funded by paid tiers, ads, data, or acquisition — each has a different cost to you.

## 6. Rights: free to use is not free to sell

Before you publish or sell something made with a free tool, answer all of these:

- What do the **plan terms** say about commercial use?
- What does the **model licence** say? Some open-weight models carry non-commercial or use restrictions.
- Does the free plan create a **watermark**, require attribution, or limit resolution or length?
- Are your generations **public** by default? Can you delete them from a gallery?
- Does the provider claim a broad licence to your inputs or outputs?
- Do you need to disclose AI use on a platform, in a book, or to a client?
- Are there third-party rights in the output — logos, characters, lyrics, a real person's likeness, or a recognizable style?
- Does your employer or client contract allow AI processing at all?

Keep a simple record for anything you publish: tool, plan, model if shown, date, prompt summary, and licence reviewed. This is not a legal shield, but it is better than trying to remember six months later.

The free-tier listing in this guide is not a licence opinion. If a use is important, ask the provider or a qualified professional.

## 7. Quality and safety checks

Free does not mean trustworthy. Before you rely on output:

- **Open the primary source** for every fact, quote, citation, statistic, or legal or medical claim.
- **Check the math yourself** or with a calculation tool you trust.
- **Run the code** and review it. A coding assistant can introduce vulnerabilities or dependency risks.
- **Read the image or video** for garbled text, extra fingers, misleading context, and accidental trademarks.
- **Listen to the audio** for pronunciation, consent, and synthetic artifacts.
- **Check accessibility.** A generated caption may be wrong; a generated document may have poor structure; a video may lack sufficient contrast.
- **Ask a second person** before publishing anything consequential.

Never use a free AI tool for diagnosis, treatment, triage, medication dosing, crisis counseling, legal decisions, or financial decisions. Those require a human professional and an appropriate system.

## 8. Red flags in the wild

- A site that asks you to download an installer from a file locker or a random "AI bundle" link.
- "Lifetime access" to a commercial model that is otherwise subscription-only, at a suspiciously low price.
- A Chrome extension or browser add-on that asks to read and change all your data on every site, for a simple task.
- An AI tool that asks for your API key, seed phrase, bank login, or remote access to your computer.
- A "free" chat service that asks for a phone number and a card before you can type a single prompt.
- A model download from a random repository with no checksum, licence, or source.
- An email claiming your account will be deleted unless you log in through a link. Go to the official site yourself.
- A free trial that requires you to invite contacts or post publicly to unlock features.
- A prompt that tries to get you to reveal a password, API key, or someone else's data. Do not comply.

## 9. A safe first experiment

Use this mini-protocol for any tool you want to try:

1. **Write the job in one sentence.** "Transcribe a 10-minute public podcast." "Make a 2-paragraph product description from these public specs."
2. **Read the free limits.** Choose the smallest plan that can complete the test.
3. **Use invented or public material.** No patient data, no client files, no unpublished manuscript.
4. **Set a timer.** Fifteen to thirty minutes is enough to learn the shape of a tool.
5. **Check the output.** Verify facts, rights, and usability. Note what needed fixing.
6. **Export your work.** Keep a local copy.
7. **Decide: keep, park, or delete.** If you keep the account, set a review date and note the billing terms.

That is the whole method. It is less exciting than a list of 169 tools, and it is what keeps the tools useful.

## 10. When a free tier changes

It will happen. Here is a calm response:

- **Confirm the change on the official source.** Social posts and screenshots may be wrong or out of date.
- **Export your data before making a decision.** Access can disappear faster than expected.
- **Check whether the change affects rights you already relied on.** Plan terms at the time of creation may matter, but do not assume.
- **Move one workflow at a time.** Do not migrate your entire business in a panic.
- **Update your copy of this guide.** Corrections belong in `data/categories/*.json`; the safety notes here are hand-written.
- **Tell someone.** If a tool is used by a team or classroom, share the change and the new limits.

The goal is not to find a free tool that never changes. It is to keep enough copies, skills, and options that a change is an inconvenience rather than a crisis.

## The pocket version

Before you sign up:

**Read the offer. Protect the data. Check the rights. Keep an exit.**

Before you publish:

**Open the source. Check the licence. Add a human.**

Before you trust a health, legal, or money answer:

**Stop. Use a qualified professional.**
